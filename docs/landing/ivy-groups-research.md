# Ivy groups: research for the Ivy League Charter

Compiled on 2026-09-29. The full data, including each institution's city, state, grad flag, note and source, is in `ivy-groups-research.json`.

Each list follows **one** stated definition, with no blending. For the Public Ivies, only the named main campus counts.

**Grad flag:**
- **true**: a range of master's programmes open to outside international applicants.
- **limited**: one or a few niche master's programmes, and no MBA.
- **false**: nothing an outside applicant can apply to now.

## 1. Ivy Plus (non-Ivy members): 4

**Definition:** Chetty, Deming & Friedman (Opportunity Insights), *Diversifying Society's Leaders?*, NBER WP 31492 (2023, rev. Aug 2025).

> "Ivy-Plus colleges (the eight Ivy League colleges, Chicago, Duke, MIT, and Stanford)... these twelve colleges"

Source: [paper (PDF)](https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/CollegeAdmissions_Paper.pdf), Introduction, p. 1 · [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31492)

| Institution | City, State | Grad |
|---|---|---|
| The University of Chicago | Chicago, IL | true |
| Duke University | Durham, NC | true |
| Massachusetts Institute of Technology | Cambridge, MA | true |
| Stanford University | Stanford, CA | true |

**Why this definition:** it is the admissions-research definition that the 2023 press coverage of legacy admissions cites.

**Not used:** the [Ivy Plus Libraries Confederation](https://ivpluslibraries.org/about/) has 13 members. It is the same set plus **Johns Hopkins**. Adding JHU is a business decision, and it would mean switching to this definition as a whole.

## 2. Public Ivies: 30 (Greene & Greene, 2001)

**Definition:** Howard R. Greene & Matthew W. Greene, *The Public Ivies: America's Flagship Public Universities* (Cliff Street Books, 2001), ISBN 0-06-093459-X.

The list is transcribed from [Wikipedia: Public Ivy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Ivy) and corroborated by [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/blog/public-ivy-schools-and-little-ivies/). I have not inspected the book itself. All 30 are grad **true**.

- **Northeastern:** Penn State (University Park, PA) · Rutgers–New Brunswick (NJ) · Binghamton University, SUNY (Vestal, NY) · UConn (Storrs, CT)
- **Mid-Atlantic:** William & Mary (Williamsburg, VA) · University of Delaware (Newark, DE) · University of Maryland, College Park (MD) · University of Virginia (Charlottesville, VA)
- **Western:** University of Arizona (Tucson, AZ) · UC Berkeley · UC Davis · UC Irvine · UCLA · UC San Diego (La Jolla) · UC Santa Barbara · CU Boulder (CO) · University of Washington (Seattle, WA)
- **Great Lakes & Midwest:** Indiana University Bloomington · Miami University (Oxford, OH) · Michigan State (East Lansing) · Ohio State (Columbus) · UIUC · University of Iowa (Iowa City) · University of Michigan (Ann Arbor) · University of Minnesota Twin Cities · UW–Madison
- **Southern:** University of Florida (Gainesville) · University of Georgia (Athens) · UNC Chapel Hill · UT Austin

**UC campuses:** the Greenes name six UC campuses: Berkeley, Davis, Irvine, LA, San Diego and Santa Barbara. They do **not** name Santa Cruz, Riverside, Merced or UCSF.

**Branch campuses:** these are excluded unless named:
- Rutgers–Newark and Rutgers–Camden
- Penn State Commonwealth campuses and Penn State World Campus
- UW Bothell and UW Tacoma
- UM-Dearborn and UM-Flint
- other University of Minnesota campuses
- Ohio State and UConn regional campuses
- Miami University regionals
- other IU campuses

**Not used:** Moll, *The Public Ivys* (1985). His original eight are:
- William & Mary
- Miami University
- "University of California" (the whole system)
- University of Michigan
- UNC Chapel Hill
- UT Austin
- University of Vermont
- University of Virginia

He also lists nine "worthy runners-up": CU Boulder, Georgia Tech, UIUC, New College of Florida, Penn State, Pitt, Binghamton, University of Washington and UW–Madison.

It is not used because it names the UC system rather than campuses, and because it has two tiers.
