1b · Trending in 2026

Same rail behaviour · trend-forward, not category-safe

What the type press actually says about 2026: neo-grotesques have displaced geometric sans in premium work, editorial serifs are having their strongest run in decades, and variable fonts with expressive axes are the thing being celebrated. The award lists — TDC, Typographica, Okay Type — crown mostly commercial faces (Edgar, Delusse, Pennyroyal, Altesse) that cost real licensing money and cannot be loaded here, so this rail is the free, loadable end of the same trend. The two most-referenced commercial faces right now are Neue Montreal and Editorial New; Switzer and Instrument Serif below are their closest free stand-ins.

5 · What I'd pick, and why

One recommendation, two fallbacks
Big Shoulders

Recommended · headlines

Every firm in the table sets headlines in a wide or geometric sans — Space Grotesk, Outfit, Satoshi, Geist, Poppins, Inter, Rubik, Montserrat. Eight firms, one silhouette. Nothing in the category is condensed.

  • Condensed is the empty lane, the same way green was in colour
  • It matches your logo's own tall narrow caps, so lockup and headline finally belong together
  • Narrow letters mean a ₹3,00,000 headline fits on one line instead of three
  • Free, on Google Fonts, seven weights, works in Docs and PDFs

Fallback A · Archivo Black

If condensed reads too editorial for you. Wide, extremely heavy, immovable — mass instead of height. Also unused in the category. The trade: it eats horizontal space, so headlines have to be shorter.

Fallback B · Bebas Neue

Closest of all to the lettering already in your logo, and the safest visual match. The catch: it is one of the most-used display faces on the internet, so it buys you the logo match and none of the distinctiveness.

Trending vs durable

Rail 1b is what the type press is excited about in 2026 — and that is exactly the risk. Clash Display, Bricolage and Instrument Serif are all over new brands right now, which means they will read as "rebranded in 2026" by 2029. A study-abroad firm signing five-year guarantees should not date that fast.

If you want trend-forward anyway, the two that survive best are Clash Display (bold enough to carry the mark) and Cabinet Grotesk (heavy, editorial, less saturated than Clash).

What stays either way

  • Body: Archivo — quiet, wide range, pairs with all three above
  • Figures: IBM Plex Mono for every rupee, EMI and certificate ID
  • Never: Montserrat, Poppins, Inter — the three that make you look like everyone else

Worth noticing

Click through Ambitio, AECC, GradRight and Edvoy above. They are different companies with different colours that still feel like one company, because the typography is the same silhouette. That is the whole argument for condensed.
