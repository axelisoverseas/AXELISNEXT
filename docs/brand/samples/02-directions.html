The same page, three ways

Identical copy, layout, type and mark in all three — only the colour changes, so what you are judging is the colour and nothing else. Every palette below clears WCAG AA on its own ground. Direction A is the recommendation; B and C are real alternatives, not straw men.

A · Axis Green on greenbar ledger

Recommended
Why. No competitor in the audit uses green as a primary — it is the one open lane. Green reads institutional and money-adjacent without reading corporate-blue. The ledger ground is the accounting stock figures have been ruled onto for a century, which is exactly the claim Axelis is making. Risk: green can drift agricultural or eco if the value ever lightens; hold it dark.

B · Oxblood on warm bone

Alternative
Why. Oxblood is the colour of law reports, university crests and bound volumes — the most academically credentialled palette of the three, and equally unoccupied in the category. Risk: it runs close to the crimson of several university brands, so lockups sitting beside a partner university logo can read as borrowed authority. It is also warmer and more traditional, which suits PhD and Executive MBA buyers more than undergraduates.

C · Ink and brass, no hue at all

Alternative
Why. The most severe option: a near-black and a paper grey, with brass as the only colour on the page. Nothing in the category is achromatic, and it puts every bit of emphasis on typography and figures. Risk: it is unforgiving — with no brand hue, a single badly-set page has nothing to fall back on, and partner logos, university crests and payment marks all fight it. It also gives Instagram nothing to work with.
